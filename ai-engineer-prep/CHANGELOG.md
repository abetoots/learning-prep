## First pass (2026-10-14)

**Why no deadline and no budget.** The goal is durable fundamentals, not a date. Framework has no budget anywhere; the one in `../interview-prep/` exists because a deadline makes the budget real whether you compute it or not. Without a date there is no total to hit, and a budget with nothing behind it is a quit point. Cadence still binds — study days, the review day, the two-week keep/cut test — and the stop condition is the competency list.

**Why the target performance is teach-it-cold plus a blank-file build.** The owner wants knowledge that survives whichever path follows — interview, transition, teaching. Those are three different exams, and a line written to cover all three decays into a topic (§3.1). The framework's own transfer check (§5 step 9, §7) is explaining it cold against hard questions; the interview form is a subset of that, the transition carries it, teaching *is* it.

**Why Track C routed per item.** §2 puts a new engineering discipline in Track C, but the mixed-track rule applies: API design, retries, state machines, tenancy, tracing are Track A for this candidate and are tested, not relearned. The LLM-specific items are C. Python is the open question — the field's tooling is Python and the evidence is TypeScript — and the blank-page pass decides it, not the owner's estimate.

**Evidence strength.** The role definition rests on primary 2025 voices (Huyen/Orosz's *AI Engineering* excerpt, swyx's RedMonk interview) and on Anthropic's own careers taxonomy (Applied AI as a hiring category distinct from research). Demand magnitude is **unverified**: the Stanford AI Index 2026 and PwC 2026 PDFs failed to fetch for two legs; Lightcast-via-AI-Index figures (agentic-AI postings 0.06% → 0.23% of US postings, 2024→2025) came through secondary summaries only. One leg's "3.2 open roles per qualified applicant" and its hiring-loop descriptions had no URL and are not counted. The recommendation stands on candidate fit, not on a market ranking — and the panel's falsifier is a posting sample for the target market that requires model-training experience.

**Known gaps in the key.** The HF Agents Course grades agents and tool use; it is thin on retrieval quality, serving/cost, and observability. Those lines (#6, #9, #10) lean on running code and Huyen's text, which explains but does not grade. The escalation — a paid human-graded evals cohort — is named in the sketch with its trigger, so it isn't bought on day one.
